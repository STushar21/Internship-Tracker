Memory tiles

A grid of nine tiles lights up in a sequence, and you repeat it back in order. Every round is a new random sequence, one tile longer than the last. You have three hearts: each mistake costs one, and when they're gone the run ends. It measures short-term visual memory, a task type used in some cognitive assessments. It doesn't build finance knowledge.

Play

How to play

Watch the tiles light up, then tap them in the same order. A correct round makes the next sequence one tile longer. A wrong tap costs a heart and repeats the same length with a new sequence. On a keyboard, keys 1 to 9 tap the tiles like a keypad: 1 is the top-left tile and 9 the bottom-right.

Scoring

Your score is the number of tiles you recall correctly, in order, across the whole run, including the correct tiles before a mistake. You start with three hearts and the run ends when the third one breaks. Sequences start at 3 tiles and can grow to 15.

Best practice

  • Group the sequence into chunks of two or three tiles, and recall each chunk as a path or a shape, not as separate tiles.
  • A tile that lights twice in a row is two steps. It's the easiest place to lose count.
  • Wait for 'Your turn' before you tap, and take a breath before you start.
  • After a mistake the next sequence is new, so don't try to recall the old one. Reset and watch fresh.
