Gamified / Cognitive

What to expect, not what to study

This category can't be drilled like the others — it's testing attention, memory, and decision-making patterns, not finance knowledge. This page is a briefing, plus two illustrative mini-games to show the format.

What these actually are

A growing number of firms — including JPMorgan, Blackstone, and Standard Chartered — use gamified psychometric assessments (often built by providers like Pymetrics) alongside or instead of traditional numerical/verbal tests. These usually take 15–25 minutes and consist of a series of short mini-games measuring things like risk tolerance, memory, attention, and reaction time.

Unlike numerical or verbal reasoning, there's no "correct" way to play these games. Your responses are typically compared statistically against a model built from existing employees, or used to build a broader behavioral profile — there's no single "right" personality, and different roles may even look for different trait profiles.

Which firms use which specific games changes over time, so treat "JPMorgan/Blackstone/StanChart" as examples of the type of firm using this format, not a guarantee of exactly what you'll see.

How to actually approach it

  • Don't try to "game" the games by guessing what trait looks good — the models are generally resistant to obvious gaming, and trying too hard to appear a certain way tends to produce inconsistent, unnatural responses that stand out more than genuine ones.
  • Respond naturally and at a normal pace — reaction-time-based games measure genuine processing speed, not a clever strategy.
  • Don't overthink risk-based games — a wide range of risk appetites is generally fine, since firms aren't looking for one "correct" amount of risk tolerance.
  • Take them somewhere quiet, undistracted, and when you're not fatigued — since these measure real-time responses, tiredness and interruptions genuinely affect the results.
  • If one game feels like it went badly, don't dwell on it — these are typically evaluated in aggregate across the full set of games, not pass/fail individually.
