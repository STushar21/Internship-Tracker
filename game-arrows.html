Arrows

Sixty seconds of left-or-right decisions. You follow the centre arrow and ignore the others, and its colour tells you whether to go with it or against it. It measures reaction speed and accuracy under a switching rule, a task type used in some attention and reaction-time assessments. It doesn't build finance knowledge.

Play

How to play

Five arrows appear. Look only at the centre one. If it's black, press the way it points. If it's red, press the opposite way. The four arrows around it are always black and only there to distract you. The next arrows appear as soon as you answer.

Scoring

Each correct answer scores 1 and each wrong answer costs 2, so guessing never pays. You get as many answers as you can fit into 60 seconds, so speed matters too. After a wrong answer there's a 0.4-second pause before the next arrows.

Best practice

  • Decide from the centre arrow's colour first, then its direction. Say 'same' or 'opposite' to yourself.
  • Ignore the outer arrows. They pull you towards their own direction, which is the wrong way on half the trials.
  • Aim for 90% accuracy or better. Each mistake costs two answers' worth of points, so slowing slightly to avoid errors usually pays.
  • On a phone, rest your thumbs over the two buttons. On a Mac, keep your fingers on ← and →.
